## Rounded subgroup shares: never invent exact counts

Secondary groups are motorcyclists,pedestrians,pedalcyclists using each report's labels. Table 7 shares are rounded whole percentages, not exact counts. For totalN and printed percentagep, allow integer counts in the closed interval [ceil(N*max(0,p-0.5)/100),floor(N*min(100,p+0.5)/100)]. Including both half-percentage endpoints is conservative across tie-rounding conventions, not a claim about NHTSA's undisclosed rounding rule. Require each interval nonempty and the sum of all five lower counts<=N; omitted other/unknown person types have nonnegative remainder. Tighten each upper bound to atmostNminus the other four lower bounds. These are marginal feasible count bounds; do not multiply N*p and label the result observed fatalities.

For each subgroup/state/denominator retain lower/upper rate bounds and ordinal-placement bounds including ties: best=1+number of other states whose lower rate strictly exceeds this state's upper rate; worst=51-number whose upper rate is strictly below this state's lower rate. Bounds include any tie ordering and need not be simultaneous across all groups. Retain complete guaranteed same/reversal/unresolved pair accounting under subgroup-count ranges plus VMT display-precision sensitivity. Inference is conditional on printed totals/shares and chosen rounding intervals; it does not certify person-level ascertainment or exact travel exposure.

Classifications changed from 2020(vehicle types) and 2022(motorized bicycles). Keep year-specific labels and analyze denominator contrasts within each year. Do not pool subgroup trends or claim harmonized cross-year incidence. Licensed drivers and vehicle miles do not measure pedestrian/cyclist person-distance; population does not measure traveler exposure. Fatalities occur in a state and may involve nonresidents, so per-population rates are not resident death risks. No individual safety, causality, legal compliance, clinical, true-risk or realized-benefit claim.
